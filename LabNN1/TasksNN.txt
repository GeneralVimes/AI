Assignments for Independent Work on the Topic: "Neural Networks"

1.  Create a network designed to solve the assigned problem.
2.  Prepare training and test data for it. The distribution of data in the
    samples must be balanced so that each case corresponding to each activated
    output neuron is represented in approximately equal quantities.
3.  Train the network to perform the task.
4.  Input two different sets of data into the network and export its states to
    Excel.
5.  Build a graph of the output neurons' activation for the trained network,
    varying the value of one input neuron while keeping the others fixed.
6.  Export the trained network as an object (e.g., for use in buildFromObject).
7.  Modify the network architecture (add or remove hidden layers, or change the
    number of neurons in them), ensuring that it remains capable of solving the
    task.
8.  Build an analogous graph for the modified network and export it as an object
    and to Excel (using 2 sets of input data).

Problems for Independent Neural Network Training

1.  Product Sign: Given 3 numbers. Determine whether their product will be
    positive or negative.
2.  Smallest of Four: Given 4 numbers: a, b, c, d. Determine which one is the
    smallest.
3.  Count Negatives: Given 4 numbers. Determine how many of them are negative
    (there are 5 possible answers: 0, 1, 2, 3, or 4).
4.  Proximity to Average: Given 5 numbers: a, b, c, d, e. Determine which of the
    numbers c, d, or e is closest to the arithmetic mean of a and b.
5.  Largest Pair Sum: Given 4 numbers: a, b, c, d. Determine which of the 6
    possible sums of two of these numbers is the largest.
6.  Triangle Existence: Given 3 positive numbers. Determine if they can be the
    sides of a triangle (Yes or No).
7.  Margin of Error Sum: Given 3 numbers: a, b, c. Determine which of the three
    cases applies to the sum a + b: it differs from c by less than 10%; it is
    greater than c (taking into account the 10% margin); or it is less than c
    (taking into account the 10% margin).
8.  Sorting Order: Given 3 numbers: a, b, c. Determine how they should be
    arranged in ascending order: abc, acb, bac, bca, cab, or cba.
9.  Longest Segment (3 points): Given 6 numbers representing the coordinates
    of 3 points on a plane (A, B, C). Determine which of the three segments —
    AB, AC, or BC — is the longest.
10. Proximity Zones: Given 5 numbers: the coordinates of 2 points on a plane and
    a positive number d. Determine the distance category between these points:
    closer than d; closer than 2d; closer than 3d; or further than 3d.
11. Longest Segment (4 points): Given 8 numbers representing the coordinates
    of 4 points on a plane (A, B, C, D). Determine which of the six segments —
    AB, AC, BC, AD, BD, or CD — is the longest.
12. Triangle Type: Given 6 numbers representing the coordinates of 3 points on a
    plane. They can form an obtuse, right, or acute triangle. (Consider a
    triangle "right-angled" if it has an angle between 82 and 92 degrees). Train
    the network to determine this.
13. Coordinate Sectors: Consider a coordinate plane divided into 8 parts by the
    axes and the lines y = x and y = -x. Given the coordinates of a point
    (x, y), determine which of these 8 sectors the point is in.
14. Nearest Neighbor: Given 8 numbers representing the coordinates of four
    points on a plane: A, B, C, D. Determine to which of the three points (A, B,
    or C) point D is closest.
15. Rectangle Perimeter: Given 8 numbers representing the coordinates of 4
    points (A, B, C, D) that are vertices of a rectangle. Determine the order in
    which these points appear along the perimeter if moving clockwise:
    ABCD, ABDC, ACBD, ACDB, ADBC, or ADCB.
16. Segment Intersection: Given 8 numbers representing the coordinates of 4
    points (A, B, C, D). Determine which case occurs: segments AB and CD
    intersect; segments AC and BD intersect; or neither pair intersects.
17. Point in Two Circles: Given 8 numbers: x_1, y_1, r_1, x_2, y_2, r_2, x, y.
    The first six numbers define two intersecting circles. Determine if the
    point (x, y) is: only in the first circle; only in the second; in the
    overlapping area of both; or outside both (4 possible locations).
18. Point in Three Circles: Given 11 numbers:
    x_1, y_1, r_1, x_2, y_2, r_2, x_3, y_3, r_3, x, y. The first nine numbers
    define 3 non-intersecting circles. Determine if point (x, y) is: inside one
    of the circles or outside all of them (4 possible outcomes).
19. Point in Triangle/Convexity: Given 8 numbers representing the coordinates
    of 4 points (A, B, C, D). Determine which of 4 cases occurs: point A lies
    inside triangle BCD; point B lies inside triangle ACD; point D lies inside
    triangle ABC; or all points form a convex quadrilateral.
20. Binary Modulo 5: The network input receives 30 zeros and ones representing
    the binary notation of a natural number N. Activate one of the five output
    neurons corresponding to the remainder of N divided by 5.
